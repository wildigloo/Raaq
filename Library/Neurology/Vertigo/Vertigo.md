# Vertigo
*Last Updated by Adem Berat Dalkilic on 8/11/26*

## Definition
Vertigo is the sensation of spinning of oneself (internal vertigo) or of the surroundings (external vertigo) while stationary. It can be caused by dysfunction of either the inner ear, causing peripheral vertigo, or dysfunction of the central vestibular system, causing central vertigo. 

## Differential Diagnosis
*Centrally, stroking tummies gives me a migraine; Peripherally, Men with lymph in lab vests have herpes, amin Loopy; Orthostatic hypotension*

1. Central: cerebellum, brainstem, vestibular nuclei dysfunction
	1. Ischemia and/or hemorrhage of the vertebrobasilar circulation (stroke/TIA) - **acute vestibular syndrome**
		- Lateral medullary syndrome (Wallenberg)
		- Vertebrobasilar insuffienciency
		- Cerebellar stroke
		- Brainstem ischemia (basilar artery occlusion, vestibular nuclei stroke)
	2. Posterior fossa tumors (schwannomas, meningiomas); may cause increased ICP, resulting in cerebellar tonsillar herniation - **chronic vestibular syndrome**
	3. Migraine - **episodic vestibular syndrome**
2. Peripheral: vestibulocochlear nerve, semicircular canal dysfunction
	1. Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo: episodic, triggered vertigo; confirm diagnosis with Dix-Hallpike maneuver
	2. Vestibular neuritis
	3. Labyrinthitis
	4. Meniere disease
	5. Medications: aminoglycosides, furosemide
	6. Perilymphatic fistula
	7. Herpes zoster oticus (Ramsay Hunt syndrome)
3. Mimicks
	- Orthostatic presyncope
	
## Classifying Vertigo
### Central vs Peripheral
- Cerebellar signs suggest central (dysmetria, dysarthria, severe ataxia); also dysphagia and diplopia suggest central
- Unidirectional nystagmus suggests peripheral; multidirectional or isolated torsional or vertical strongly suggest central
- Hearing loss suggests peripheral; hearing loss is rare in central
- Focal neurologic deficits are usually present in central and absent in peripheral
- HINTS exam:
	- Head impulse: positive in peripheral (absent vestibulo-ocular reflex in peripheral)
	- Nystagmus: see above
	- Test of skew: positive in central (pupil skew present in central)
### Classification into Vestibular "Syndromes"
- Acute vestibular syndrome: acute onset of continuous vertigo, gait instability, nystagmus, and nausea; may be worsened, but not triggered, by movement
- Episodic vestibular syndrome: recurrent episodes of vertigo associated with gait instability and nausea that last seconds to hours; may be triggered or spontaneous
