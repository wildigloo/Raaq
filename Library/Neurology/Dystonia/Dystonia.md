# Dystonia [1]
*Last Updated by Adem Berat Dalkilic on 8/11/26*

## Classification
- Primary dystonia: dystonia is the sole neurological sign; **usually insidious, sometimes inherited, may come into sight during execution of a specific, well-learned action**
	- Oppenheim dystonia (DYT1 gene): begins at childhood (13 years avg onset)
- Secondary dystonia: dystonia caused by secondary cause; **sudden onset after environmental trigger, along with other neurological signs**
	- Medications
	- Stroke/lesionsn of basal ganglia (putamen or globus pallidus)
- Dystonia plus syndromes: no etiology or neurodegeneration, but there are neurological symptoms other than dystonia as well
	- Dopa-responsive dystonia: usually in children, but can start at any age; in adult onset, it manifests as pure parkinsonism; patient responds well to anticholinergics
	- myoclonus dystonia
	- rapid-onset dystonia-parkinsonism

## Presentation
Involuntary writhing or twisting precipitated by specific voluntary movements like walking or writing. Torticollis presents as an uncontrollable head-turning. Task-specific, focal limb dystonia presents as hand cramping or involuntary wrist/finger spasms when writing. Oromandibular dystonia: night-time bruxism following dental procedures, facial trauma, or temporomandibular joint dysfunction. Spasmodic dysphonia: strained voice. Dystonic tremors have a null point, usually, and can be associated with myoclonus and usually disappear in sleep; dystonia may cause hypertrophy of affected muscles. Cervical dystonia, head tremor can decrease with head in specific position.

## Differential Diagnosis
*Oppenheimer medicates after his stroke; dystonia + parkinson lysis RBCs!
- Primary dystonia
	- Oppenheim dystonia
- Medication effect
- Stroke, or post-stroke spasticity
- Dystonia plus syndromes
	- Dopa-responsive
	- Rapid onset dystonia-parkinsonism
	- myoclonus dystonia
- Parkinson-plus syndromes 
- Neuroacanthocytosis
	- Choreoacanthocytosis
	- McLeod disease
- Batten disease (and other LSDs)
