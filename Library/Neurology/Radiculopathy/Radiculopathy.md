#Radiculopathy
**Last Updated by Adem Berat Dalkilic on 8/11/26**

## Definition: dysfunction at the level of a nerve root, including motor and sensory deficits

## Typical presentation
- Sharp, radiating electric shock like pain and paresthesias in dermatomal distribution; may be associated with weaknes sin a myotomal distribution; may also observe paraspinal muscle spasms.

## Differential Diagnosis
- Structural impingement (most common)
	- Disc herniation
		- L4-L5 (L5 radiculopathy): neurogenic claudication
		- L5-S1 (S1 radiculopathy): neurogenic claudication
		- C6-C7 (C7 radiculopathy): headache
		- C5-C6 (C6 radiculopathy): headache
	- Spinal stenosis
	- Osteophyte (and other degenerative conditions of the spine)
	- Trauma
- Infectious
	- Bacterial or fungal
	- CMV
	- Epidural abscess
	- HSV
	- Lyme disease
	- Syphilis
- Inflammatory
	- Autoimmune
	- Chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyradiculoneuropathy
	- Guillain-Barre syndrome
	- Sarcoidosis
- Neoplastic
	- Epidural metastases
	- Leptomeningeal carcinomatosis
	- Neoplasms

## Roots and Corresponding Exam Findings: (Sensory loss, motor loss, reflex loss)
- C5: lateral arm; rhomboids, infraspinatus, deltoid, biceps; biceps, brachioradialis
- C6: lateral hand and forearm; biceps, deltoid, biceps, pronator teres; biceps, brachioradialis
- C7: second, third digits; triceps, pronator teres, extensor carpi radialis; triceps
- C8: medial hand; intrinsic hand muscles, finger flexors and extensors; none
- L3: anterior thigh, medial knee; iliopsoas, quadriceps, hip adductors; patellar
- L4: lower medial leg; quadriceps, hip adductors; knee
- L5: lower lateral leg, dorsum of foot; tibialis anterior, gluteus medius; none
- S1: lateral and plantar foot; gluteus maximus, gastrocnemius, hamstrings; ankle

## Extra Localizing Maneuvers
- Straight let raise: suggests lumbar radiculopathy
- Spurling: suggests cervical radiculopathy
- Shoulder abduction relief: hand is placed ipsilateral to cervical radicular symptoms on head; positive if pain improves

## Confirming the Diagnosis
- EMG and NCS are best suited
- MRI is useful for identifying spinal epidural abscess, cauda equina syndrome, osteomyelitis, and metastases
- If MRI is contraindicated, order a CT myelogram
