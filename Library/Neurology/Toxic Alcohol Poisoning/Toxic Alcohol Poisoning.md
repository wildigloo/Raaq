# Toxic Alcohol Poisoning
*Last updated by Adem Berat Dalkilic on 8/11/26*

Suspect toxic alcohol poisoning when observing an elevated osmolar gap and an elevated anion gap metabollic acidosis

## Determining Type of Alcohol Poisoning
### Methanol
- Optic neuropathy (blurred vision, scotoma, blindness)
- Papilledema
- Peripapillary retinal edema
- Afferent pupillary defect
- Kussmaul breathing
- Tachycardia
- Abdominal pain
- Hemorrhagic gastritis

### Ethylene glycol
- Accumulation of oxalic acid causes deposition of calcium oxlate crystal in renal tubules causing acute kidney injury
- Stages
	1. Neurologic stage (first 12 hours)
		- Altered mental status
		- Ataxia
		- Seizures
		- Nystagmus
	2. Cardiopulmonary stage (12-24 hours)
		- Tachypnea
		- Tachycardia
		- ARDS and/or circulatory collapse
	3. Renal stage (24-72 hours)
		- Flank pain
		- Hematuria
		- Oliguria/anuria
		- Twitching, stridor, Troussea, Chvostek (hypocalcemia from oxalic acid combining with calcium to form calcium oxalate)

### Isopropyl alcohol
- Altered mental status
- Ataxia
- Headache
- Hypotonia
- Seizures
- Nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain
- Fruity, sweet-smelling breath (from acetone accumulation)
- Hemorrhagic gastritis and/or hematemesis
- If aspirated: pulmonary edema, hemorrhagic tracheobronchitis
- Respiratory depression and/or hypotension

## Treatment
- Methanol (bootleg alcohol, windshield washer fluid, solvents) and Ethylene Glycol (antifreeze, deicing fluids, plastic ingredient)
	- Antidote: alcohol dehydrogenase inhibitors (e.g., fomepizole)
	- Enhance elimination:
		- Normalize pH with sodium bicarbonate
		- Consider hemodialysis
- Isopropyl alcohol (aka rubbing alcohol; disinfectant, solvent, antiseptic) 
	- Supportive care: monitor for GI bleed and hypotension
	- Antidote and enhanced elimination usually not required
- Diethylene glycol (brake fluids, lubricants, paints, ink)
